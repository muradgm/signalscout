# SignalScout Logo Directions

## Goal

Create an original SignalScout logo system that feels:

- sharp
- premium
- simple
- modern
- trustworthy

The target blend is:

- Stripe-like brand sharpness
- Mercury-like elegance and pacing
- Resend-like cleanliness

The final logo should not feel playful, generic, soft, or overly "AI startup."

## Direction 1: Signal Frame

### Summary

A minimal geometric mark built from a framed signal path.

### Visual idea

- a compact rectangular or rounded-rect frame
- inside it, one clean rising or shifting line
- the line suggests signal detection, review, or judgment

### Why it fits

- feels product-grade and infrastructure-adjacent
- visually stable and easy to place in nav
- works well with a clean wordmark
- closest to the Stripe-style simplicity target

### Brand meaning

- visible signals
- structured review
- evidence contained inside a decision system

### Best use

- navbar lockup
- favicon/app icon
- social avatar
- product loading mark

### Risk

- if drawn too literally, it could look like a generic chart icon

### Design guardrail

Keep it abstract enough to feel branded, not like analytics software.

## Direction 2: Scout Lens

### Summary

A minimal circular or softened-square mark suggesting focus, inspection, and signal scanning.

### Visual idea

- a lens-like ring or nested shape
- subtle directional cut or offset inside the shape
- can hint at "scouting" without becoming a magnifying glass

### Why it fits

- communicates inspection and clarity
- gives us a softer premium direction than pure geometry
- works well if we want a more elegant Mercury-like lockup

### Brand meaning

- focused review
- selective attention
- operator judgment

### Best use

- hero wordmark lockup
- app icon
- motion animation seed for hero visuals

### Risk

- if too rounded or too open, it may feel generic or consumer-app-like

### Design guardrail

Keep the construction crisp and deliberate, not friendly or bubbly.

## Direction 3: SS Monoline

### Summary

An abstract monogram built from two clean strokes that imply "S" and "S" without spelling it too obviously.

### Visual idea

- one continuous or near-continuous line system
- subtle dual-curve or mirrored construction
- minimal enough to pair with a strong wordmark

### Why it fits

- more ownable if executed well
- more brandable than a generic icon
- could feel very strong in a Stripe-inspired identity system

### Brand meaning

- SignalScout as a system, not just a descriptive tool
- quiet confidence
- strong identity with low visual noise

### Best use

- wordmark companion
- favicon
- embossed / subtle brand moments

### Risk

- hardest to get right
- can become too clever or too logo-designer-coded

### Design guardrail

If it needs explanation, it is too complicated.

## Wordmark Direction

For all three directions, the wordmark should follow the same logic:

- primary emphasis on `SignalScout`
- high contrast
- tighter spacing
- cleaner and more deliberate than the current version
- not overly wide
- not playful

Recommended posture:

- custom-tuned wordmark feel
- modern sans base
- slightly refined shaping around `S`, `g`, or `t` if needed

## Recommended Order

### First choice

`Signal Frame`

Why:

- strongest fit with the current product
- easiest to keep clean and premium
- best match for Stripe-inspired simplicity

### Second choice

`Scout Lens`

Why:

- elegant and flexible
- pairs well with the Mercury influence

### Third choice

`SS Monoline`

Why:

- potentially most ownable
- but also easiest to over-design

## Motion Notes

The future hero/logo motion should feel:

- precise
- smooth
- restrained
- intentional

Good motion ideas:

- line reveal
- soft scan sweep
- subtle signal pulse
- layered parallax shift

Avoid:

- bouncing
- glowing tech-orb behavior
- exaggerated morphing
- flashy AI-style shimmer

## Recommendation

Proceed with:

1. `Signal Frame`
2. `Scout Lens`
3. `SS Monoline`

Create first-round explorations in black/ink only before color.

That keeps the mark honest and lets us judge shape quality before polish.

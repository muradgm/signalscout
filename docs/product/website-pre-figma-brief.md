# SignalScout Website Pre-Figma Brief

Last updated: 2026-04-11

## Purpose

This document is the source of truth for the first SignalScout marketing website exploration before any Figma files are created.

It exists to keep the website:

- aligned with actual product truth
- visually distinctive without drifting into generic AI SaaS design
- clean, simple, and elegant
- honest about current product scope and maturity

## Product Truth To Protect

SignalScout should be presented as:

- a single-operator lead review workspace
- for local-service business websites
- that helps an operator inspect signals, review an audit, generate outreach, and decide whether to send

The website should not present SignalScout as:

- a broad CRM
- an autonomous outbound platform
- a team collaboration suite
- a large analytics product
- a fully mature reply-operations system

### Core promise

SignalScout helps an operator decide:

- which local-service websites are worth contacting
- why they are worth contacting
- what grounded outreach to send next

### Secondary capability rule

Sending is real, but it is secondary.

Replies exist, but they are not part of the core website promise.

## Audience

The website should primarily speak to:

- a founder or operator doing outbound themselves
- a small internal growth or research workflow
- a user who wants clearer lead qualification and stronger outreach grounding

The website should feel strongest for:

- dental
- clinic-style
- trust-sensitive local-service businesses

It may gesture toward adjacent local-service categories, but it should not sound universal.

## Positioning Direction

### Recommended positioning line

SignalScout is a lead review and outreach recommendation workspace for local-service websites.

### Tone

The website should feel:

- restrained
- confident
- editorial
- precise
- warm without becoming soft

The website should not feel:

- loud
- futuristic for its own sake
- flashy
- growth-hacker styled
- enterprise-generic

## Recommended Visual Direction

Use an **Evidence Desk + Operator Console hybrid**.

### Evidence Desk

Contributes:

- editorial calm
- high trust
- layered evidence presentation
- a tactile, reviewed feel

Visual cues:

- paper-like surfaces
- soft contrast blocks
- annotation marks
- evidence highlights
- confidence and status labels

### Operator Console

Contributes:

- modern product clarity
- stronger hierarchy
- workflow structure
- next-action emphasis

Visual cues:

- clean grid
- deliberate spacing
- compact status chips
- precise card framing
- sharp section rhythm

### Combined result

The final direction should feel like:

- a beautiful decision workspace
- not a sales automation dashboard
- not a glossy abstract AI landing page

## Homepage Narrative

The homepage should tell one clean story:

1. inspect the website
2. understand the signals
3. review an audit
4. generate grounded outreach
5. let the operator decide what happens next

The site should lead with judgment, evidence, and clarity.

It should not lead with automation volume, campaigns, or reply workflow depth.

## Recommended Homepage Structure

Aim for a 6-8 section homepage.

### 1. Hero

Goal:

- communicate the core wedge immediately

Should include:

- one sharp headline
- one supporting line
- a product-led visual or framed workspace preview
- one primary CTA
- one secondary CTA

Working headline direction:

Lead review and outreach recommendations for local-service websites.

Supporting copy direction:

Review website signals, see a grounded audit, generate a better outreach draft, and decide what to send next from one operator workspace.

### 2. Proof Of Workflow

Show the core workflow in one line:

- snapshot
- signals
- audit
- outreach
- operator decision

This section should feel like a calm progression, not a busy feature grid.

### 3. Why It Is Credible

Focus on:

- evidence-led recommendations
- confidence and ambiguity handling
- local relevance and booking/contact quality
- operator review instead of blind automation

This is where SignalScout earns trust.

### 4. Workspace Preview

Show:

- queue view
- lead detail review view
- evidence framing
- next-action clarity

The preview should look selective and calm, not crowded.

### 5. Best-Fit Use Cases

Speak to:

- founder-led outbound
- solo operator research
- small internal lead review workflows

Keep this grounded in current strength.

### 6. Capability Boundary Strip

Short, restrained section that says:

- sending supported
- replies visible
- both secondary to the main review workflow

This keeps product truth honest without hiding real capability.

### 7. Final CTA

Simple, confident close:

- request demo
- see the workflow
- talk through fit

## Visual System Guidance

### Layout

- strong container rhythm
- generous whitespace
- structured editorial blocks
- product visuals framed as important artifacts, not decorative screenshots

### Color

Avoid:

- purple-heavy AI gradients
- cold SaaS blues as the whole identity
- flat black-on-white minimalism with no warmth

Prefer:

- warm neutrals
- bone, stone, sand, paper, graphite
- one deep accent such as forest green, ink green, or dark olive
- one restrained signal accent for confidence, status, or review emphasis

### Typography

Avoid default-feeling stacks.

The site should use a more intentional pairing:

- a refined serif or soft editorial display face for emphasis
- a crisp sans serif for interface and body text

The result should feel:

- modern
- intelligent
- composed

Not:

- luxury-brand theatrical
- startup-template generic

### Motion

Use motion sparingly and purposefully:

- layered reveal on page load
- subtle panel movement
- staggered evidence-card entrance

Do not rely on:

- shiny hover gimmicks
- excessive floating animation
- decorative motion without meaning

## Product Visual Framing

Website product visuals should emphasize:

- reviewed evidence
- status and confidence
- recommendation clarity
- one obvious next action

They should not emphasize:

- fake analytics breadth
- large empty sidebars
- team dashboards
- automation control centers

Product screenshots should be:

- cropped intentionally
- annotated selectively
- shown as believable working surfaces

## Image Strategy

### Hard rule

All final website imagery should be original and generated exclusively for SignalScout.

Do not use:

- stock photography
- borrowed illustration systems
- generic AI character art
- unmodified third-party visual packs

### Image direction

Generated imagery should support the product story, not distract from it.

Best directions:

- abstract signal landscapes
- evidence-board compositions
- locality-inspired texture systems
- interface-adjacent editorial visuals

Images should feel:

- exclusive
- calm
- intelligent
- slightly tactile

Images should not feel:

- sci-fi
- whimsical
- mascot-driven
- over-rendered
- visually louder than the product itself

## Design Do / Don't

### Do

- make the site feel selective and intentional
- show the product early
- prioritize narrative clarity over feature count
- use elegant framing and spacing
- make confidence, evidence, and review visible themes

### Don't

- imply full automation
- imply broad platform breadth
- overemphasize replies
- use decorative visuals that could belong to any AI company
- make the homepage feel like a generic B2B template

## Pre-Figma Deliverables

Before Figma starts, create:

1. a reference board for hero mood
2. typography options
3. color/material options
4. screenshot framing examples
5. original image direction examples for SignalScout-only generated visuals
6. one homepage wireframe
7. two higher-fidelity visual concept lanes

## Recommendation

Proceed with:

- one homepage wireframe
- two visual concept lanes

Primary lane:

- Evidence Desk + Operator Console hybrid

Secondary lane:

- a slightly more atmospheric Local Signal Atlas variant, but still restrained

If the two concepts diverge too much, prefer the one that makes the product feel:

- clearest
- most trustworthy
- most specific
- least generic

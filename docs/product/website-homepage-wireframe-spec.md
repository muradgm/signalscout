# SignalScout Website Homepage Wireframe Spec

Last updated: 2026-04-11

## Purpose

This document is the section-by-section homepage blueprint for the first SignalScout marketing website.

It is meant to let a designer move directly into Figma without inventing:

- the page order
- the narrative arc
- the layout priorities
- the screenshot strategy
- the visual emphasis rules

## Page Goal

The homepage should make SignalScout feel like:

- a beautiful decision workspace
- for reviewing local-service business websites
- with evidence-led audit and outreach guidance
- inside one clean operator workflow

The page should not make SignalScout feel like:

- a generic AI sales tool
- a large outbound platform
- a CRM
- a reply-operations system
- a broad analytics product

## Page Structure

The homepage should use 7 sections in this order:

1. Hero
2. Workflow Rail
3. Credibility / Why It Works
4. Workspace Preview
5. Best-Fit Use Cases
6. Secondary Capabilities
7. Final CTA

## Global Layout Rules

### Container behavior

- use one consistent page container system
- keep section widths disciplined
- let the hero and workspace sections feel slightly wider than the others

### Spacing

- generous vertical spacing
- calm pacing between sections
- no compressed feature-grid feeling

### Grid

- desktop: 12-column grid
- tablet: 8-column grid
- mobile: stacked single-column logic with selective paired cards

### Visual rhythm

Alternate between:

- product-led sections
- editorial explanation sections

This should create a calm read, not a wall of equally weighted cards.

## Section 1: Hero

### Goal

Tell the whole product story in one glance:

- what it is
- who it is for
- why it matters

### Layout

Desktop:

- left: headline, supporting line, CTAs, small proof row
- right: framed hero composition using product UI plus original SignalScout-generated imagery support

Mobile:

- headline and copy first
- CTAs second
- product composition below

### Content

#### Headline

Working direction:

Lead review and outreach recommendations for local-service websites.

Alternative direction:

Review the site. See the signals. Send better outreach.

#### Supporting copy

Working direction:

SignalScout helps a single operator inspect website signals, review a grounded audit, generate outreach, and decide what to send next from one clean workspace.

#### CTA structure

Primary CTA:

- Request a demo

Secondary CTA:

- See the workflow

#### Small proof row

Use 3 short proof chips:

- Evidence-led review
- Local relevance and confidence
- Audit to draft to send

### Hero visual

Use a composed artifact, not a full raw screenshot.

Recommended composition:

- one large crop of the lead detail review workspace
- two smaller layered inserts showing evidence, confidence, or recommendation states
- subtle original generated image texture behind or around the product frame

### Hero design notes

- this section should feel premium and calm
- the product should be visible immediately
- no huge abstract illustration should overpower the UI

## Section 2: Workflow Rail

### Goal

Show the product sequence clearly and simply.

### Layout

Horizontal sequence on desktop:

- snapshot
- signals
- audit
- outreach
- decision

Vertical stack on mobile.

### Content structure

Each step should have:

- short label
- one-line explanation
- simple icon or visual marker

### Recommended labels

- Inspect the site
- Surface the signals
- Review the audit
- Generate the draft
- Decide the next move

### Design notes

- keep this elegant and restrained
- avoid "process infographic" energy
- it should feel like a quiet product rail

## Section 3: Credibility / Why It Works

### Goal

Earn trust by showing why the product is more grounded than generic AI messaging.

### Layout

Use a 2-column intro followed by a 2x2 proof-card grid on desktop.

Mobile:

- intro first
- proof cards stacked

### Intro copy

Suggested headline:

Grounded recommendations, not generic automation.

Suggested supporting line:

SignalScout focuses on local relevance, booking and contact quality, evidence, and operator review so the next action is clearer and more defensible.

### Proof cards

Use 4 cards:

1. Evidence-led recommendations
2. Confidence and ambiguity handling
3. Local relevance and commercial fit
4. Operator review before send

### Card content shape

Each card should include:

- short title
- one concise sentence
- one small support detail or annotation

### Design notes

- cards should feel like reviewed artifacts
- use subtle emphasis markers and status chips
- this section should visually bridge editorial tone and product UI tone

## Section 4: Workspace Preview

### Goal

Make the product feel real, usable, and desirable.

### Layout

Desktop:

- one dominant workspace frame
- 2 or 3 supporting crops around it
- optional annotation captions

Mobile:

- stacked main frame
- supporting crops below

### Required visual content

Show:

- lead queue context
- lead detail evidence area
- recommendation or action framing
- one example of audit or outreach review

### Screenshot rules

- use intentional crops
- avoid showing empty or low-value chrome
- prioritize evidence, labels, confidence, and next-action moments
- keep screenshots believable and product-truth aligned

### Supporting copy

Suggested headline:

One workspace for review, judgment, and action.

Suggested body:

See the lead, inspect the signal quality, review the audit, and generate a stronger draft without bouncing between disconnected tools.

### Design notes

- this should be the visual anchor of the page
- the UI should feel beautiful enough to carry the product promise

## Section 5: Best-Fit Use Cases

### Goal

Clarify who the product is for right now.

### Layout

Use 3 side-by-side use-case cards on desktop.

Stack on mobile.

### Recommended cards

1. Founder-led outbound
2. Solo operator research
3. Small internal review workflows

### Supporting note

Add a restrained sub-note that the product is strongest today for:

- dental
- clinic-style
- trust-sensitive local-service businesses

### Design notes

- do not sound universal
- avoid "for every GTM team" language
- keep the cards elegant and specific

## Section 6: Secondary Capabilities

### Goal

Acknowledge real functionality without turning it into the headline.

### Layout

One horizontal strip or compact 2-card band.

### Content

Card 1:

- Sending supported
- Keep it framed as part of the operator workflow

Card 2:

- Replies visible
- Keep it framed as secondary, not central

### Supporting copy

Suggested line:

SignalScout supports sending and reply visibility, while keeping the core product centered on evidence-led review and outreach decisions.

### Design notes

- this section should be visually quieter than the hero and workspace preview
- think "honest capability note," not "feature expansion"

## Section 7: Final CTA

### Goal

Close with calm confidence and one clear next step.

### Layout

Centered CTA block with restrained supporting visual texture.

### Content

Suggested headline:

See how SignalScout sharpens the next outreach decision.

Suggested supporting line:

Get a closer look at the workflow, the evidence framing, and how the product fits a focused local-service outbound process.

Primary CTA:

- Request a demo

Secondary CTA:

- See the workflow

### Design notes

- the page should end cleanly
- avoid big footer-style feature overload before the CTA

## Navigation Guidance

Keep top navigation minimal.

Recommended items:

- Product
- How it works
- Fit
- Request demo

Avoid:

- many top-level categories
- fake platform breadth pages
- large marketing-site nav systems

## Footer Guidance

Keep the footer compact.

Suggested contents:

- brand mark
- short one-line descriptor
- primary links
- legal/privacy links if needed

## Motion Guidance

### Allowed motion

- subtle fade and rise for section entry
- slight stagger for proof cards
- gentle parallax or depth only if very restrained
- hover response for cards and CTAs

### Disallowed motion

- floating everything
- dramatic scroll theatrics
- shiny hover gimmicks
- product mockups flying around

## Original Image Guidance

All final imagery must be original and generated exclusively for SignalScout.

### Recommended homepage image placement

- hero support texture/composition
- subtle credibility-section background moment
- optional CTA-section texture

### Not recommended

- giant decorative illustration blocks
- image systems louder than the product UI
- human stock-photo replacement imagery

## Figma Deliverable Checklist

The first Figma pass should include:

1. desktop homepage wireframe
2. mobile homepage wireframe
3. hero composition rough
4. workflow rail rough
5. credibility card system rough
6. workspace preview framing rough
7. use-case card system rough
8. CTA block rough

## PM Review Checklist

The homepage concept passes PM review if:

- the wedge is obvious in the hero
- the product is visible early
- the site does not imply broad automation or platform breadth
- sending stays secondary
- replies stay secondary
- the page feels elegant and specific rather than generic
- the imagery plan remains SignalScout-original
- a designer could build from the spec without inventing the structure
